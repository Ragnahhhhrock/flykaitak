# Personas

Five reader types. They are working hypotheses built from what the sim offers (modes, languages, share tools); check them against GA4 audience data and the `/stats/` page after 30 days.

## 1. Sim Sam, the flight-sim enthusiast
- **Who:** 25 to 55, plays desktop flight sims, follows realism and procedures. Mostly English-speaking (Australia, UK, US, Europe, Asia).
- **Wants:** a hard, authentic approach, aircraft with their own flight models, weather and night flying, tips to improve a score.
- **Pain:** easy "arcade" landings, vague instructions, sims that need big downloads. Fly Kai Tak runs in the browser.
- **Finds us via:** search ("Kai Tak approach simulator", "IGS 13 approach"), Reddit and forum posts, YouTube, Discord.
- **Reads:** step-by-step approach guides, aircraft deep-dives, scoring explained, controls cheat sheets.
- **Action we want:** fly the approach, beat a score, try another aircraft or weather.

## 2. Wing, the Hong Kong local and diaspora reader
- **Who:** 30 to 65, grew up under the Kai Tak flight path or has family who did. Reads Cantonese first, English second. Lives in Hong Kong or abroad.
- **Wants:** to see 1998 Hong Kong again: Kowloon City shop signs, the checkerboard, Star Ferry, neon, estates, taxis and minibuses.
- **Pain:** few places show that Hong Kong accurately, and little of it is in written Cantonese.
- **Finds us via:** Facebook and Instagram, Threads, shares from friends and family, Hong Kong history groups.
- **Reads:** the Cantonese version of the Real Hong Kong and Kai Tak Story posts, photo-led pieces.
- **Action we want:** open the sim, look around in Plane Spotter or the helicopter tour, share with family.

## 3. History Harry, the aviation-history buff and plane spotter
- **Who:** 35 to 70, remembers or studies the jet age: the 747, Concorde, Kai Tak's last years. Collects facts, enjoys a good story and accurate detail.
- **Wants:** well-sourced history, aircraft details, airport layout (stands, apron, tower, nullah), the day-to-day of a busy airport.
- **Pain:** myths and sloppy facts. Notices errors.
- **Finds us via:** search, aviation history communities, links from other aviation sites.
- **Reads:** Kai Tak Story posts, aircraft pages, apron and layout pieces, spotting guides.
- **Action we want:** play Plane Spotter and the aircraft he likes, send corrections, share the post.

## 4. Casual Casey, the curious browser gamer
- **Who:** 16 to 30, mostly on a phone. Lands on a shared score, report card or replay video and wants a quick, fun challenge.
- **Wants:** a short game with a clear goal ("land it, get a rank") and something to show friends.
- **Pain:** long tutorials, installs, complicated controls.
- **Finds us via:** TikTok, Instagram, shared report cards and replay videos, friends.
- **Reads:** short, visual posts: challenges, phone controls, "how to get Captain", crash stories.
- **Action we want:** play one landing, save a replay video, share the score.

## 5. Eddie, the educator and creator
- **Who:** teachers, aviation students, streamers, YouTubers and history channels who need an accessible example of a tricky approach or a visual for a video.
- **Wants:** something that explains the approach clearly, can be screen-recorded, and credits the source properly.
- **Pain:** inaccurate visuals, unclear licences, no way to cite.
- **Finds us via:** search, Reddit, social shares, creator communities.
- **Reads:** build-log and "how it was made" posts, the data and sources, behind-the-scenes pieces, replay video and sharing guides.
- **Action we want:** link to the site, use the replay video tool, credit Fly Kai Tak and the data sources.
