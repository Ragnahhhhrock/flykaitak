The high score table had two perfect 100s and a long run of scores in the 90s. That was too easy, so landings are now scored much harder, and the table starts again.

What changed

The old score only looked at the moment of touchdown, in wide bands. The new score is still out of 100, but every part is a smooth curve: full marks only inside a tight window, then a straight-line fall to nothing. It also scores how you flew the approach and the rollout, not just the touchdown.

  • Touchdown (51 points): sink rate 16, touchdown point 14, centreline 8, airspeed 8, alignment with the runway 5, wings level 4.
  • Approach (30 points): glidepath 14, track 8 and speed control 8, measured from 3.5 nm out until just before the flare.
  • Rollout (15 points): staying on the centreline 8, and stopping with runway to spare 7.
  • A tail strike still takes points off, and the score is rounded down.

Why a 100 is now rare

A 100 needs every part inside its full-marks window on the same flight: a sink rate of 120 fpm or less, touchdown 280 to 450 m past the threshold, within 1.5 m of the centreline, within 2 kt of the target speed, under 1° of bank, a glidepath held to about 6 m, and a straight rollout. The autopilot scores about 80 to 89 in most weather, so beating it takes real skill, and the storms and typhoon make it harder again.

After each landing the result screen names the two parts that lost the most points. Fix those first on the next attempt.

Ranks and the new table

  • Captain 90+, First Officer 78+, Second Officer 62+, Flight Engineer 45+, then Cadet. Grades are A 90+, B 78+, C 62+, D 45+.
  • Landings scored the old way are not shown any more, so everyone starts from the same line. The starter scores are lower to match.
  • Only hand-flown IGS 13 approach landings are ranked, as before.
