Three pieces of the airport have been redrawn from a 1990s aerial photo and the airport plans: the shape of the passenger terminal, the drainage channel across the apron, and the fuel tank farm.

The terminal is no longer a box

The pier used to be one long rectangle. The apron face of the slab is now set back, and lower gate blocks stick out from it to the stand line. The jet bridges come out of these blocks.

  • South-west end: a long gate block with the jet bridges of stands 1 to 4. The control tower stays on the slab behind it.
  • Stand 5: a small gate pod of its own.
  • North-east end: a second long gate block for stands 6 to 8, then the main terminal block.
  • Each gate block has a glazed lounge band at jet bridge level, lit at night, over a dark ground floor.

The nullah across the apron

The storm-water channel now crosses the apron. It comes out from under the north-east end of the terminal and runs parallel to the runway, between the stands and the maintenance apron.

  • An open walled channel, cut into the apron, with water, grass verges and a railing on both sides.
  • Three taxi bridges cross it, with yellow-and-black kerbs and yellow edge lines.
  • It widens into a grassed oval basin, then goes into a culvert under the cargo apron.
  • The culvert comes out through a headwall at the head of the inlet.

The open channel and its verges are not tarmac. A jet that taxis into it is off the pavement, like the grass: it stops at the edge at walking pace and crashes at speed. Taxi over the bridges.

The tank farm

The fuel tanks have moved to where the aerial photo shows them: just past the north-east end of the terminal, on its landside half and in line with the building. There are now two rows inside a low bund: three big, squat tanks on the apron side and four slimmer, taller tanks behind them toward the road. The low white building beyond them has been moved clear so they can be seen from Kowloon City.

Sources: a 1990s aerial photo of Kai Tak, the Hong Kong Kai Tak Airport map, Technocratic Aviation's Kai Tak plan and the World Flyer plan at Kai Tak Sports Park.
