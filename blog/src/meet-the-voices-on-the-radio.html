The radio now has four voices, one for each speaker on frequency. Here is who you hear, and when, on a typical Runway 13 approach.

Who speaks

  • Hong Kong Approach (119.1) clears you for the IGS 13 approach with the current QNH, and asks you to report the checkerboard.
  • Kai Tak Tower (118.7) takes you at the hand-off, gives you the wind, and clears you to land when the runway is clear.
  • You have your own voice for the readbacks, which are automatic.
  • Other crews on Approach chatter in a different voice: cleared approaches, holds at waypoint Golf and hand-offs to the tower.

What you will notice

  • Frequencies, wind and QNH are read out digit by digit, the way controllers say them.
  • If the runway is not clear, Tower tells you to continue the approach, and if it still is not, to go around.
  • In free flight Tower gives you the take-off clearance, then hands you to Approach.
  • In Plane Spotter and Spectator you hear the tower and approach traffic.
  • The first time a line is spoken it can take a moment to arrive. Each line is kept, so the same call is quick the next time. If a voice does not arrive in time, the browser voice reads that call instead.

Controls

  • R turns the radio off, and Sound off mutes the voices.
  • On the helicopter tour the radio stays text only, so the guide is the only voice.

The autopilot still lands in every weather preset, and flying with no input still ends in a crash.
